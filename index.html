          Mission 4 · 35 minutes

          🧪 Grammar Lab: Present simple — the basics

          First time learning the present simple? No problem. Take it step by step. Today we focus only on affirmative and negative sentences. Questions come in the next class.

          🌍 So, what is the present simple?

          We use the present simple to talk about things that are generally true, habits, routines, and likes or dislikes. It is not only for things happening right now.

          🔁

          Habits & routines

          Things we do regularly.

          I play video games after school.

          = This is a regular activity.

          ❤️

          Likes & dislikes

          Things we enjoy or don't enjoy.

          We love listening to music.

          = This is our preference.

          🌎

          Facts & general truths

          Things that are generally true.

          Dogs enjoy playing.

          = A general statement.

          Remember: “I am playing a game right now” is a different tense. Today, we focus on general activities and preferences: “I play games every day.”

          🧱 Build an affirmative sentence

          Start with who + verb + the rest of the idea.

          🧍

          I / You / We / They

          Use the verb in its basic form.

          I like music.
          You play tennis.
          We enjoy cooking.
          They watch movies.
          👤

          He / She / It

          Usually add -s or -es.

          He likes music.
          She plays tennis.
          Otto enjoys running.
          My dog watches TV.
          💡 Easy memory trick: With he, she, it (or one person/thing), the verb usually gets an extra ending. “I like” but “she likes.”

          ✍️ How do we spell the verb?

          ➕ -s

          Most verbs

          like → likes
          love → loves
          play → plays
          run → runs

          ➕ -es

          Often -sh, -ch, -s, -x, -o

          watch → watches
          catch → catches
          go → goes
          do → does

          🔄 -y → -ies

          Consonant + y

          study → studies
          try → tries

          But vowel + y: play → plays, enjoy → enjoys.

          One important irregular verb: have → has. Example: “She has two hobbies.”

          🚫 Make a negative sentence

          Use a negative when something is not true, or when someone does not like or do something.

          🙋

          I / You / We / They

          don’t + base verb

          I don’t like golf.
          They don’t play tennis.
          We don’t enjoy hiking.
          🧑

          He / She / It

          doesn’t + base verb

          He doesn’t like golf.
          She doesn’t play tennis.
          Otto doesn’t enjoy cold weather.
          🚨 Watch out! After doesn’t, use the basic verb. Say “She doesn’t like golf,” NOT “She doesn’t likes golf.” The -s is already carried by does.

          Quick comparison: ✅ Otto likes cats? (affirmative form) → ❌ Otto doesn’t like cats. (negative form)

          ⚡ Practice 3 — Fix the mistake

          There are 10 rounds. Read each sentence, say the correction aloud, then reveal the answer and rule. Try to correct it before opening the card!

          1. He like playing soccer.
          Reveal answer

          He likes playing soccer. Add -s with he.

          2. She doesn’t likes golf.
          Reveal answer

          She doesn’t like golf. After doesn’t, use the base verb.

          3. They doesn’t enjoy cooking.
          Reveal answer

          They don’t enjoy cooking. Use don’t with they.

          4. My brother watch TV every night.
          Reveal answer

          My brother watches TV every night. Watch ends in -ch, so add -es.

          5. I likes listening to music.
          Reveal answer

          I like listening to music. With I, use the base verb.

          6. We doesn’t play board games.
          Reveal answer

          We don’t play board games. Use don’t with we.

          7. My sister studys English.
          Reveal answer

          My sister studies English. Consonant + y changes to -ies.

          8. The dog don’t like cats.
          Reveal answer

          The dog doesn’t like cats. The dog = it; use doesn’t + base verb.

          9. My parents enjoys hiking.
          Reveal answer

          My parents enjoy hiking. My parents = they; use the base verb.

          10. He doesn’t watches soccer.
          Reveal answer

          He doesn’t watch soccer. The main verb stays in the base form after doesn’t.
